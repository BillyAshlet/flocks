    One possible application

    Echo Cartography

    Not the last stage of flocks: one of the uses I have in mind for a swarm like it, from a past project of mine.

    A swarm that maps a place it cannot see, using nothing but the moments it almost hits something.

    The idea

    A fish that swerves around a wall has, for a moment, worked out that something is there and how far away it is. Normally that is thrown away once the fish has turned. Here each device keeps it and sends only that: “I almost hit something, here.” Many near misses add up to a map of places no camera has seen.

    A ray that passes through open water counts against anything being there. Moving things, like the predators or the devices themselves, keep getting erased from the map this way; rock cannot be erased, because nothing ever passes through it. So the seabed separates from the fish without any recognition.

    What you are looking at

    The swarm explores a canyon: blue devices, turning gold when they panic. Red predators patrol it, and the swarm flees and scatters from them. The panel on the right is the terminal: it rebuilds the map from what the swarm sends, tracks how much has been covered, sends devices toward unexplored edges, and finally calls them back.

    The line at the bottom compares what the swarm sends with what streaming every sensor would cost.

    Drag to orbit. Press Space to pause and resume.

    Where this comes from

    Echo Cartography is a past project of mine, made as a team project at the Manycore spatial intelligence workshop in August 2026. It is here as one place a swarm like flocks could be put to use. The code is moved over almost as it was, with its own engine, only recolored and trimmed for this page. The full project is at manycore.billyashlet.com.
